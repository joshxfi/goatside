---
title: Sign-in and security
description: How the one account signs in, stays signed in, and resists guessing.
---

A Goatside deploy has exactly one account, set by the username and password in its
environment. There's no sign-up page, no user table and no password reset. To change
your password, change `GOATSIDE_PASSWORD` in Vercel and redeploy.

## Staying signed in

Signing in sets a cookie that lasts 30 days and renews itself while you keep using the
app, so a phone you use every week stays signed in. The cookie is `HttpOnly` and, in
production, `Secure`.

There are no stored sessions. A cookie is a signature over your username, password and
`SESSION_SECRET`, so changing any of the three signs out every device at once. That is
also how you sign out a lost phone. Pages it kept for offline use are deleted the next
time it opens Goatside online, and a kept page is never shown once it's 30 days old.

## Guessing passwords

Goatside records every sign-in attempt before checking it, so a burst of parallel
guesses can't slip past the limits:

- **5 failed attempts per IP address** in 15 minutes.
- **25 failed attempts in total** in 15 minutes, from anywhere.

A flood of bad guesses could then lock you out too, so Goatside trusts browsers you've
signed in from. They get a separate year-long cookie that skips both limits. A trusted browser
that fails more than 10 times in a day loses that trust, and changing your password
untrusts every browser.

During an attack, the limits can still block a browser that has never signed in. If that's a
concern, [Vercel Firewall](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-firewall) rate limits can sit in
front of the sign-in page.

## The agent's actions

When you ask the agent to change something, it can't do it alone. The server works out
exactly which entries would change and shows you that list. Nothing changes until you
confirm. The server signs each approval with a key derived from `SESSION_SECRET`, so the
browser can't forge one. You can undo every change.
